    About entropy

    Entropy is an estimate of how many guesses an attacker would need to crack your password. It is calculated from length and character variety, but real-world cracking also depends on dictionary words, patterns, and leaked password lists. Treat this number as a rough guide, not a guarantee.

    Learn About Password Security

    What makes a password strong?

    A strong password is long, random, and unique. Length is the single most important factor — every extra character multiplies the number of possible combinations. Randomness means no dictionary words, names, or predictable patterns. Uniqueness means you never reuse it on another site.

    Why password length matters

    Attackers often use "brute force" — trying every possible combination. Each extra character increases the search space exponentially. For example, an 8-character password with mixed characters has trillions of combinations, but a 16-character one has quadrillions of times more. Modern GPUs can try billions of guesses per second, so short passwords fall quickly no matter how "complex" they look.

    Why password reuse is dangerous

    If you use the same password on multiple sites and one of them gets breached, attackers will try that password on your email, banking, and social accounts. This is called credential stuffing. A single breach can compromise every account that shares the password.

    Why unique passwords are important

    Unique passwords contain the damage of a breach. If a site you use is compromised, the attacker only gets access to that one account — not your entire digital life. Combined with a password manager, unique passwords are easy to maintain.

    What password managers do

    Password managers securely store all your passwords behind one master password. They generate strong random passwords for each site, autofill them for you, and sync across your devices with end-to-end encryption. You only need to remember one strong master password. Popular options include Bitwarden, 1Password, and KeePass.

    Why MFA / passkeys improve security

    Multi-factor authentication (MFA) requires a second proof of identity beyond your password — like a code from an authenticator app or a hardware key. Even if your password is stolen, an attacker still can't get in without the second factor. Passkeys go further: they replace passwords entirely with cryptographic keys stored on your device, making phishing and credential theft nearly impossible.
